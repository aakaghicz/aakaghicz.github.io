title: 'Why I Started Writing'
date: 2026-10-30

I have always loved reading. Books, articles, essays, biographies, and sometimes just a few sentences that make me stop and think. I enjoy discovering how other people see the world, how they make sense of their experiences, and how they put into words things I may have thought about myself but never quite managed to express.

But there is something I have been thinking about lately. Many of us spend a great deal of time reading, yet relatively few of us take the time to write. We collect knowledge, ideas, observations, and experiences, but most of them remain somewhere in our thoughts. Some become stories we tell our friends or family. Others slowly fade, and with time, even we begin to forget the details.

I have often wondered what happens to all those thoughts and experiences if we never write them down.

For years, I have wanted to write a book. Not necessarily about one particular subject, but about life, people, places, science, and everything I have learned along the way. The problem was always the same: where would I even begin? There was simply too much to say. So, like many things we promise ourselves we will do one day, the book remained an idea.

Perhaps this website is my way of finally beginning.

I sometimes say that I have been fortunate to have lived a very interesting life. I use the word fortunate carefully, because interesting does not always mean happy. Some experiences have been wonderful, others extremely difficult, and a few have changed the course of my life completely.

I survived the Srebrenica genocide and became a refugee, unable to return to my hometown. Years later, while pursuing my PhD in Japan, I experienced the devastating earthquake of 2011. I have moved to different countries on my own several times, each time having to build a new life, find my place in an unfamiliar environment, and learn how to feel at home again. I also experienced the premature birth of my son during a period of significant work-related stress.

These are only a few chapters of my life. There are many others, some joyful, some painful, some perhaps quite ordinary to anyone else, but meaningful to me.

I do not mention these experiences because I want my life to be defined by hardship. Quite the opposite. I have also had extraordinary opportunities, met wonderful people, traveled to fascinating places, and spent years doing work that I genuinely love. I have laughed a lot, made mistakes, taken risks, changed my mind, and occasionally surprised myself with the decisions I have made.

But all these experiences, good and bad, have left their marks on me. They have influenced how I think, how I understand people, what I value, and how I approach life.

And I think it is finally time to start unfolding some of these stories.

I am writing primarily for myself. I want to organize my thoughts, revisit memories, and perhaps understand certain experiences differently now that some time has passed. I am curious to see what I might discover about myself in the process. Sometimes we think we understand an experience until we try to explain it in words.

At the same time, I have decided to make these writings public. This is not an easy decision, because writing about science is very different from writing about yourself. In academic work, we are trained to be objective, precise, and careful about what we claim. Personal writing asks for something else. It requires a certain openness, including the willingness to admit that we do not always have the answers.

Over the years, through teaching and research, I have had the opportunity to encourage many young people to pursue science, challenge themselves, and believe that they can achieve more than they initially imagined. That has been one of the most rewarding parts of my professional life.

Here, I would like to continue sharing what I have learned, but in a different way. Not only about becoming a better scientist, but also about becoming a more thoughtful, curious, and independent person. I want to write about ideas that interest me, places that have stayed with me, people who have influenced me, and questions that I am still trying to answer.

I do not expect these writings to follow a particular order. Some may be personal stories, others reflections on science, society, travel, or everyday life. I may occasionally return to something I wrote earlier and see it differently. That is part of the process.

Perhaps, one day, these notes will become the book I have been thinking about for so long. Or perhaps they will simply remain a collection of thoughts and memories. Either way, I will be glad that I finally started.

And if something you read here makes you think, reminds you of your own experiences, or gives you a different perspective, <a href="/contact">I would love to hear from you</a>. Send me a note. I am genuinely interested in how other people see the world, and whether something I have written has resonated with them.

For now, this is just the beginning.
